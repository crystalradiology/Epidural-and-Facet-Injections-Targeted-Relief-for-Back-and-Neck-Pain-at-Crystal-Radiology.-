What Are Epidural and Facet Injections?
Epidural and facet injections are procedures designed to deliver medication close to specific structures in the spine that may be contributing to pain.
Although both treatments involve an injection, they target different areas and are generally used for different pain patterns.
•	Epidural injections target the epidural space around the spinal nerves and are commonly considered for pain caused by irritation or inflammation of nerve roots.
•	Facet joint injections target the small joints between the vertebrae and may be considered when facet joint inflammation or arthritis is contributing to localised spinal pain.

Epidural Injections for Nerve-Related Pain
An epidural steroid injection (ESI) delivers anti-inflammatory medication into the epidural space around the spinal nerves.
When a spinal nerve becomes irritated or inflamed, pain may travel along the path of the nerve. Depending on the location of the affected nerve, this can result in symptoms affecting the arm, shoulder, buttock or leg.
Nerve-related symptoms may include:
•	Shooting or radiating pain
•	Tingling or pins and needles
•	Numbness
•	Burning sensations
•	Pain that travels from the back into the leg
•	Pain that travels from the neck into the arm
Conditions such as a disc bulge or herniation, spinal stenosis and other spinal changes may irritate nerve roots and contribute to these symptoms.
An epidural injection aims to reduce inflammation around the affected nerve and may provide temporary pain relief, potentially making it easier for patients to participate in rehabilitation and everyday activities.
Facet Joint Injections for Localised Back and Neck Pain
Facet joints are small joints located at the back of the spine. They help connect the vertebrae and contribute to spinal stability and movement.
These joints can become painful because of arthritis, age-related changes, injury or other degenerative conditions. Facet-related pain is often felt locally in the neck or back and may become worse with certain movements, including bending, twisting or extending the spine.
A facet joint injection may contain a local anaesthetic and corticosteroid. The local anaesthetic can temporarily numb the area, while the corticosteroid is intended to reduce inflammation.
Facet injections may have both diagnostic and therapeutic purposes.
If pain improves significantly after medication is delivered to the suspected facet joint, this may provide useful information to the treating doctor about the possible source of the pain. The injection may also provide temporary relief from symptoms. 
How Are These Injections Performed?
Image guidance is an important part of these procedures because it helps the treating specialist accurately identify the relevant anatomical structures and guide the needle into the intended location.
The general process may include:
1.	You will be positioned appropriately for the procedure.
2.	The skin will be cleaned and prepared.
3.	A local anaesthetic may be used to numb the injection area.
4.	Imaging is used to guide the needle.
5.	The medication is delivered to the target area.
6.	The needle is removed and the injection site is covered.
The exact technique varies according to the type and location of the injection.

Are Epidural and Facet Injections Suitable for Everyone?
These procedures are not appropriate for every patient. Your doctor or specialist will consider your symptoms, diagnosis, imaging findings, medical history and current medications before recommending an injection.
You should tell your healthcare team about:
•	Blood-thinning medications
•	Allergies or previous reactions to medications or contrast
•	Current infections or illness
•	Pregnancy or possible pregnancy
•	Diabetes or other relevant medical conditions
•	Previous spinal procedures
Your treating practitioner can explain the potential benefits, risks and alternatives before the procedure.
